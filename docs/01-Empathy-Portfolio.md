# Empathy Portfolio

## Background

Modern college students juggle multiple responsibilities across their academic lives: attending lectures, writing notes, tracking assignments and deadlines, preparing for exams, practicing coding skills, and seeking help when they do not understand a concept. The tools available to them are fragmented — a notes app here, a calendar there, a coding platform somewhere else, and a search engine for quick questions. This fragmentation creates cognitive overhead: students spend more time switching between tools than actually studying.

StudySphere is intended to address this fragmentation by bringing the core study workflow into a single, personalized workspace. It combines notes, a planner (timetable, exams, attendance, GPA, semester tracking, daily/weekly goals), an assignments tracker, a coding hub with live preview, coding progress tracking, analytics, an AI assistant for study help, and a social layer (friends and study groups) — all backed by a cloud database so data persists across devices.

The problem is not the lack of individual tools, but the lack of a unified, student-centric workspace that reduces friction and keeps everything connected.

## Target Users

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Primary** | Undergraduate and postgraduate students (ages 18–25) who study multiple subjects and use digital tools daily |
| **Secondary** | Self-learners and coding bootcamp participants who need practice tracking and AI-assisted explanations |
| **Context** | Students who own a laptop or smartphone, have internet access, and are comfortable with web applications |
| **Skill level** | Not necessarily technical — the interface must be usable by non-CS students as well as CS students |
| **Pain context** | Students who feel overwhelmed by deadlines, lose notes across multiple apps, struggle to track coding practice, or want quick AI help without leaving their study environment |
